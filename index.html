What are TARGET balances?

TARGET balances are the net claims and liabilities of the euro area national central banks (NCBs) vis-à-vis the ECB which arise through cross-border payments settled in central bank money. These payments are made through TARGET, the Eurosystem's payment system (ECB-inspired definition.

How to read the chart

Above zero: over time, more money has flowed into that country than out of it. Below zero: the opposite.

By design, all balances together add up to zero, once the ECB and the non-euro central banks that take part are included.

What does TARGET include?

Today, TARGET reunites several of services, known as TARGET Services:

  • T2 for large payments between banks and for the central bank's own operations
  • T2S for the cash side of securities trades
  • TIPS for instant payments, 24 hours a day

At the end of every day, all cross-border flows across these services are combined into a single balance for each central bank against the ECB.

A short history
  • 1999: TARGET is launched together with the euro, linking the national payment systems.
  • 2007–2008: TARGET2 replaces it with a single shared platform.
  • 2015: T2S starts settling securities in central bank money.
  • 2018: TIPS launches for instant payments.
  • 2023: T2 replaces TARGET2, and all services run on one consolidated platform.
  • 2025: ECMS, a common system for managing collateral, completes the set.
  • 2026: Pontes links blockchain-based platforms to TARGET.

How to read the table

Each row is a country. Each cell is the money that came into that country from the partner in the column, minus the money that went out to it, added up from 2009 to 2025, in € billion.

The last three columns compare the sum of all these flows with how much the TARGET balance actually moved from December 2008 to December 2025. The gap is the difference.

What is counted

Only data published by partner country: goods (IMF), services (Eurostat), direct investment and its income (Eurostat), shares and bonds (IMF) and bank loans and deposits (BIS).

What is in the gap?

Everything that is not published by partner: wages of cross border workers, interest and dividends, transfers such as the EU budget, derivatives, loans outside banks, errors and omissions. There are also years that a source does not publish, for example services before 2010 to 2013 and direct investment before 2013.

What it shows

Flows between the four countries are small. Most of the money goes through the rest of the euro area (financial centres like Luxembourg, Ireland, the Netherlands perhaps) and the rest of the world. The biggest direct link is Germany and Spain.
